# Taylor Riley: brand system

## One-sentence positioning

I build reliable tax software and AI development tools, connecting domain expertise, system design, and team leadership to turn complex rules into software people can verify.

## Foundation

The through-line is translating difficult domain rules into working systems. Taylor's trajectory runs from taxpayer support and hands-on tax development to Principal, Manager, Senior Manager, and Director, Tax at Taxwell (July 2026). Leadership expands the reach of the building work; it does not replace it. His distinguishing work connects tax calculations, developer context, validation, and the operational handoff.

Write for engineers, technical leaders, and domain experts who need to understand what changed and why it works. Lead with the constraint, explain the decision, and show evidence. Do not turn career exploration into public job-search positioning.

## Three core pillars

1. **Reliable software in complex domains.** Tax rules, form identity, version history, and regression evidence. Explain how ambiguity becomes explicit data and testable behavior.
2. **AI tools grounded in real systems.** Code intelligence, authoritative language rules, compiler access, and regression tooling. Show what the model can inspect and how its output gets checked.
3. **Technical leadership through useful tools.** Build the first working version, involve the people who use it, establish ownership, and make the work repeatable. Credit collaborators and distinguish building from co-building and leading.

## Voice and tone guardrails

### Do

- Open with the technical insight or tradeoff. Prefer short sentences and concrete verbs.
- Use first person for Taylor's decisions and work; use “we” only for documented collaboration.
- Explain unfamiliar terms once. MCP means Model Context Protocol; DSL means domain-specific language.
- State scope, dates, units, and limitations beside metrics. Separate a tested result from production outcomes.
- Say “built,” “co-built,” or “led” precisely. Attribute the production ingestion platform to a team.
- Use contractions and a helpful, conversational tone. Use bullets for lists.
- Show the cost of a decision as well as its benefit. An unresolved print difference matters even when calculation parity passes.

### Don't

- Use em dashes, corporate slogans, manufactured anecdotes, invented quotes, or unsupported causal claims.
- Claim that AI eliminates errors, that a dashboard caused an entire operational improvement, or that a sample proves universal correctness.
- Lead with “In today's rapidly evolving landscape,” “I'm excited to announce,” or a generic rhetorical question.
- Inflate job titles, imply sole ownership of team work, or use customer reach as personal impact.
- Publish private compensation, recruiting conversations, colleague evaluations, internal screenshots, customer data, or proprietary code.
- Reuse old blog anecdotes as biographical evidence. Existing publication is not proof of accuracy.

## Editorial vocabulary

| Prefer | Avoid |
| --- | --- |
| Build, ship, fix, test, measure | Leverage, utilize, operationalize |
| Architecture, interface, dependency, failure mode | Ecosystem, synergy, holistic solution |
| AI tools, source context, compiler, regression check | AI-powered transformation, revolutionary, game-changing |
| Reduced a named backlog from X to Y | Drove unprecedented impact, unlocked value |
| Co-built with the team | Single-handedly transformed |
| Repeatable process, clear owner | Operational excellence, thought leadership |
| Script (for a test program) | Client (when it means a test script) |
| Explain the tradeoff directly | “This isn't about X. It's about Y.” |

Established technical terms belong when they make the explanation more precise. Do not replace precision with marketing language.

## Visual language

- Neutral paper and ink, with one deliberate blue accent. Light and dark mode follow the operating system.
- Use `styles/tokens.css` for colors, system sans typography, monospace labels, spacing, and reading width.
- Text and links require at least 4.5:1 contrast; focus indicators require at least 3:1. Never convey meaning by color alone.
- Dense, scannable sections with clear headings and enough room for touch targets. No decorative motion, glass effects, gradients, or remote fonts.
- The TR monogram uses integer geometry on a 16-unit grid. Do not add tiny details or text inside it.
- Favor semantic HTML and native browser behavior. JavaScript needs a user benefit.

## Publication workflow

`/brand-post` produces local drafts for Taylor to review. It never posts, sends, commits, or deploys a draft. Drafts are ignored by git and excluded from static hosting. Date labels distinguish draft creation from publication. Do not silently make drafts public when changing the portfolio.
